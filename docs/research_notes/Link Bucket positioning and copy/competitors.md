# Competitive landscape: read-later / link-saving apps (as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-02. Google Play data (title, short description, rating, review count, in-app purchase range, last-updated date) was scraped from US/en_US Play listings that day. The "short description" is the Play `og:description` field. Download counts were only captured for some apps.

## 1. What happened to Pocket and Omnivore, and where did their users go?

### Takeaway
Mozilla shut Pocket down on July 8, 2025 and deleted its data after October 8, 2025. Omnivore shut down in November 2024 after ElevenLabs acquihired its team. That left a large pool of orphaned users. Dozens of apps, from Raindrop and Instapaper down to small indie apps, now advertise themselves directly as "Pocket alternatives." No single app took Pocket's place. Roundups agree the category "shattered" into niches.

### Cited Findings
- Mozilla: "Pocket shuts down July 8, 2025". "Users can export saves anytime until October 8, 2025, after which their data will be permanently deleted." "Annual subscribers will receive automatic refunds from July 8, 2025." Reason given: "the way people save and consume content on the web has evolved, so we're channeling our resources into projects that better match browsing habits today." It says Pocket "has helped millions save articles," with no exact number. Fakespot was shut down at the same time (July 1, 2025). — [Mozilla blog](https://blog.mozilla.org/en/mozilla/building-whats-next/)

- Omnivore: ElevenLabs acquired the team on Nov 1, 2024 to work on ElevenReader. The service shut down on Nov 15, 2024 and user data was deleted. The export window was later extended to Nov 30, 2024. ### Inferences
- The "Pocket alternative" SEO space is saturated, mostly with vendor-written listicles. Link Bucket can still mention Pocket import, but leading with "Pocket alternative" puts it in a crowded field.
- Two shutdowns in 12 months make "it won't disappear on you" and "export anytime" credible worries. Raindrop already uses the durability angle. Link Bucket can make a stronger claim: there is no server that can be shut down, and your data is already on your phone in plain files.

### Gaps
- No reliable migration statistics. None of the sources say how many Pocket users moved where. Raindrop's own page has no numbers.
- Mozilla's support KB page (support.mozilla.org/kb/future-of-pocket) did not load.

## 2. Competitor profiles

### Takeaway
The big players are all account- and cloud-based. Most charge subscriptions (Instapaper $5.99/mo or $59.99/yr; Readwise Reader $9.99–12.99/mo; Raindrop Pro around $28/yr). They compete on reading features, highlights and AI. One-time pricing, no account and widgets exist mostly on Apple (GoodLinks, Anybox) or in small Android indie apps with few reviews.

## 3. Crowded vs. open positioning angles

### Takeaway
These angles are crowded: "read it later / Pocket alternative", "save anything / bookmark everything", "highlight & annotate", "AI tagging/summaries/semantic search", "listen / text-to-speech" and "self-hosted / open source". These remain open on Android with any traction: a deliberately small queue that resists hoarding, a widget as the main interface, a one-time price with no subscription, and no server at all with plain-file backups.

### Cited Findings
- "Read it later" / "save now, read later" is used verbatim by Instapaper, Folio, Paperspan, ReaderFlow, LaterLinks, Save It Later and Pocket Links. — Play listings above
- "Bookmark everything / all-in-one" is claimed by Raindrop ("All in One Bookmark Manager") and Karakeep ("The Bookmark Everything App"). — [Play Raindrop](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.raindrop.raindropio); [karakeep.app](https://karakeep.app)
- Highlights/annotations are offered by Raindrop, Readwise, Linkwarden ("Highlights & annotations"), and Instapaper (notes). — listings above
- AI is offered by:
  - Readwise Ghostreader.
  - Karakeep (AI tagging, summaries, semantic search).
  - Linkwarden (AI tagging).
  - Fabric ("AI workspace").
  - Marqly (semantic AI search).
  - Burn 451 (AI summaries).
  - iLinkVault, TidyLinks and Smarter Bookmarks.

  — sources above
- Anti-hoarding exists but only as a gimmick:
  - Burn 451 uses a 24-hour deletion timer on iOS and Chrome only.
  - LaterLinks uses "Stop losing important links" copy, but its framing is about saving a lot of links.

  — [burn451.cloud](https://www.burn451.cloud/); [Play LaterLinks](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dci.dev.holdmylink)
- One-time pricing in the category:
  - GoodLinks: one-time.
  - Anybox: $39.99 lifetime.
  - Plinky: $159.99 one-time.

  All three are Apple-focused. Among the Android apps checked, Pocket Links Pro is an "optional subscription". — sources above

### Inferences
- "A queue, not a graveyard" differs from Burn 451. Burn 451 punishes with deletion. Link Bucket caps the queue (3 slots) and holds the overflow in "Waiting", so nothing is lost. Framing it as a calm, finite queue avoids the anxiety of Burn's "watch it burn".
- Users seem to accept the idea that people never read what they save. Burn 451 builds its pitch on that claim, though its 84% figure is unsourced.

### Gaps
- No survey data on what share of read-later users want subscriptions vs one-time pricing. The only signal is roundup phrasing about users "who hate subscriptions".

## 4. Does anyone already lead with a home-screen widget or "no account"? How do they phrase it?

### Takeaway
"No account" appears in several small Android apps, usually as a bullet lower in the description rather than as the headline. No Android link saver found leads with the widget. Where widgets are mentioned they are feature bullets: "bookmark widgets … for one-tap access to your favorite folders" and "Dashboard widgets". On Apple, GoodLinks and Anybox mention widgets as secondary features.

### Cited Findings
- Save For Later (Pleme): "No account required—simply open the app and start building your personal reading list." and "Privacy First: No sign-up or account required. Your data stays on your device for maximum privacy." — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.pleme.saveforlater)
- Pocket Links: "Use Pocket Links without internet or an account. Your bookmarks stay safely stored on your device. No ads." / "Add bookmark widgets to your Home Screen for one-tap access to your favorite folders." — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nofutureapps.pocketlinks)
- Smarter Bookmarks: "No sign-up required / No unnecessary permissions / Your data is stored ONLY on your [device]" / "Dashboard widgets for quicker access". — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.smarter.technologist.android.smarterbookmarks)
- Save It Later: "Your data stays on your device by default." — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.saveitlater.app)
- Watch Later (movies): "Works offline, no account required / Your lists live on your device." — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.watchlater.app)
- GoodLinks widget copy: "quickly access a list or open a random unread link from your home screen"; privacy copy: "nothing is shared online unless you choose to share it". — [goodlinks.app](https://goodlinks.app)
- Anybox: "Sync via iCloud. No personal data collected." — [anybox.app](https://anybox.app)
- Instapaper: "Supports widget/launcher shortcuts" (a single line). — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.instapaper.android)
- A Raindrop Play reviewer wrote "Some widgets would be nice though". — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.raindrop.raindropio)

### Inferences
- "No account" alone does not set Link Bucket apart on Android, since at least four small apps claim it. It works when combined with "no server / nothing to shut down" and plain-file backups (JSON + text) that the user can see.
- The widget-as-the-queue (thumbnails, flipping between buckets) appears to be unclaimed as the headline. Competitors treat widgets as shortcuts to folders, not as the place where you actually work through the queue.
- Pricing copy like "Pay once. No subscription." is rare on Android in this category. It is mainly a known pitch for GoodLinks and Anybox on iOS.

### Gaps
- I did not inspect screenshots, so I cannot confirm whether any competitor shows a thumbnail queue widget in its store imagery.
- I did not search Play exhaustively. The searches used were "bookmark widget", "save for later widget", "watch later links", "link saver no account", "read later" and "pocket alternative".
