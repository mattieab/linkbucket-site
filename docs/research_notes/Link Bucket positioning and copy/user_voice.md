# Voice of Customer: Read-Later / Link-Saving Apps (2025–2026)

Method note: Reddit (www and old.reddit) could not be fetched by the research tools, and `site:reddit.com` searches returned no Reddit pages. Reddit voice is therefore missing; Hacker News, MacPowerUsers forum, Product Hunt, Michael Tsai's blog, DEV Community, press, and competitor blogs were used instead. Quotes marked (HN), (MPU), etc. were extracted from fetched pages by a summarizing fetch tool; wording should be spot-checked against the link before going into final copy. Quotes marked "[search snippet]" come from search-result summaries only and are weaker.

## 1. Why people abandon read-later apps (the "graveyard" problem)

### Takeaway
The "saved, never read" pattern is the dominant, almost cliched, complaint in the category. People describe it as a graveyard, cemetery, hoarding, "bankruptcy" and guilt, and in 2025–2026 several new apps and blogs are explicitly positioning against it. Nobody provides hard statistics; it's treated as common knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- "Saved, never read. Saving is effortless and reading takes work, so queues grow until users wipe the whole list in guilt and uninstall." — [Slax blog, "Saved, Never Read: A 20-Year History of Read-It-Later Apps"](https://slax.com/blog/read-it-later-history/) (2025/26; no stats cited)
- DEV Community post headline: "Your 'Read Later' list is a graveyard. It is time to stop hoarding." Also: "We feel productive. We feel like we have learned something just by bookmarking it. But we haven't." and "I wasn't building a knowledge base. I was building a digital cemetery." — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/the_nortern_dev/your-read-later-list-is-a-graveyard-it-is-time-to-stop-hoarding-388g) (Feb, year 2025/2026; page shows "February 11")
- Commenter embernoglow: "Surprisingly, saving bookmarks is easier (ctrl+D) than opening them." Commenter ofri-peretz: "Most 'read later' tools optimize for saving speed, not for recall" — [same DEV thread](https://dev.to/the_nortern_dev/your-read-later-list-is-a-graveyard-it-is-time-to-stop-hoarding-388g)
- Pocket refugees describe "a queue that grew faster than they read it, and a vague guilt attached to opening the app"; quoted social post: "a never-ending loop of forgotten articles and abandoned dreams". The article also argues unlimited free tiers (e.g. Raindrop's "unlimited bookmarks") make abandonment worse because queues grow without friction. — [Burn451 blog, May 2026](https://www.burn451.cloud/blog/read-it-later-app) (vendor blog; quote sourced vaguely to "X and Reddit, 2025")
- Typical pattern described: install, save a flood in week one, open two months later to "200 unread items", feel guilty, archive everything — [search summary of savethenread.com](https://savethenread.com/blog/read-later-app-that-actually-works) [search snippet; page returned 503]
- Upworthy writer on Pocket: "For 18 years, Pocket helped the Internet's voracious readers—and biggest procrastinators—collect their favorite stories online." On the moment to read: "Often, that moment never came. That's not the point." Calls it "a digital magpie nest". — [Upworthy, May 28, 2025](https://www.upworthy.com/pocket-saying-goodbye/)
- Older (2020): "an archive of articles I kid myself into thinking I'll read when I'm not too busy"; "I was about ready to declare Pocket bankruptcy" — [Rebooting newsletter, Aug 4, 2020](https://rebooting.substack.com/p/one-hand-in-my-pocket) [OLDER SOURCE]
- HN users report huge hoards: "It turns out I have ~37K saves. Whoa." (bayindirh); "Yeah I have 32k saves and hit the same problems with search being extremely unreliable." (gxqoz) — [HN, May 2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44063662)
- HN user bsnnkv on Omnivore shutdown: adopt a "'read it now or read it never' (RINORIN) approach" — [HN, Oct 2024](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41985118) [OLDER, late 2024]
- HN darrmit: "Ugh this is the only read-it-later service I ever found that 'stuck'." — [HN, Oct 2024](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41985118) (shows that most don't stick)
- Instagram's Saved tab described as "where recipes go to disappear"; people "scrolling through a graveyard of saved videos" — [search summary of recipe-saver blogs, e.g. stasht.app](https://stasht.app/blog/how-to-save-recipes-from-instagram-and-tiktok) [search snippet]

### Inferences
- "Graveyard", "cemetery", "hoarding", "never read", "guilt", "bankruptcy", "200 unread" are the native vocabulary. "A queue, not a graveyard" maps directly onto words people already use; it is not an invented metaphor.
- Two camps exist: hoarders who want an archive (37K saves, "That's not the point") and people who feel guilty. Link Bucket's 3-item free plan speaks to the second; the first will object (see section 7).
- Competitors (Slax, Burn451, savethenread, readless) are converging on the same "anti-graveyard" message in 2025–26, so the idea alone won't set Link Bucket apart. The *mechanism* (a hard small cap + a widget that keeps the queue visible) is the thing to emphasize.

### Gaps
- No credible statistic on what percentage of saved items go unread; every source asserts it without data.
- No Reddit r/ADHD verbatims could be retrieved.

## 2. What Pocket and Omnivore users lost and want in a replacement

### Takeaway
Pocket shut down July 8, 2025 (export until Oct 8, then data deleted; some sources say Nov 12 for the final export window). Omnivore shut down Nov 2024 after an acqui-hire, with about 20 days' notice. The main emotional reactions: anger at short export windows, distrust of free/VC services ("not as likely to disappear on you"), wanting data you own, and a pull toward self-hosting, Obsidian, or simple durable tools.

### Cited Findings
- Pocket shutdown July 8, 2025; exports until Oct 8, 2025, after which data is "permanently deleted" — [TechCrunch, May 22, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/22/mozilla-is-shutting-down-read-it-later-app-pocket); Slax says the last export window closed Nov 12, 2025 — [Slax](https://slax.com/blog/read-it-later-history/) (dates conflict; Mozilla's announced date was Oct 8)
- "Anyone who missed it lost their reading list for good — years of saved articles, permanently deleted." — [search summary of Slax](https://slax.com/blog/read-it-later-history/) [search snippet]
- benjaminoakes: "Wallabag. It's open source and self-hostable, so it's not as likely to disappear on you." — [HN, May 2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44063662)
- bayindirh: "I want a personal box/shelf which keeps a list of what I want to read and what I have read." / "The initial idea was nice: do not lose what you want to read later (the list), keep a list of what you read." / "Permanent copies was a great add-on while it lasted too. Because I want the version I have seen." — [HN, May 2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44063662)
- kome on Pinboard: "it's very good because it's simple and hasn't changed in over 10 years." — [HN, May 2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44063662)
- trinsic2: "I imported the data into Obsidian and I now use that for information I want to save online." — [HN, May 2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44063662)
- Ben G (comment): "Anything I come across on the web and want to check out later, I Save to Pocket. It's like my own personal archive"; replacement needs: "It has to be cross-platform, 1-click to add a link, and ideally supports tags" — [Michael Tsai blog, May 2025](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/05/22/shutting-down-pocket/)
- Warner Crocker: "The read it later service became almost unusable and I had gradually moved away from my reliance on it" — [Michael Tsai blog](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/05/22/shutting-down-pocket/)
- Product Hunt Pocket thread: one user falls back to leaving "Chrome windows opened on my smartphone, during weeks or more sometimes. Messy but cross-device."; another values "no forced subscription"; "I'm really going to miss how easy and dependable it has been." — [Product Hunt, 2025](https://www.producthunt.com/p/pocket/pocket-is-shutting-down-best-read-it-later-alternatives)
- Omnivore (Oct 2024, OLDER): freeqaz: "Damn they're giving under 3 weeks to export before you lose all of your data?"; xz18r: "Just me or is that extremely short notice? That's in 2 weeks…"; dod9er: "Yes, just as it is right now I would love a cheap alternative."; guptarohit: "why good things don't last longer." — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41985118)
- MPU forum (Oct–Nov 2024, OLDER): dario: "I never trust anyone to delete my data; I'm happier doing that myself." and "Giving people just 20 days between the announcement and data deletion is absurd and highly unprofessional, though."; vikrantsingh_au: "I always wondered how this app will survive and they always insisted that this app is for long term." — [MacPowerUsers Talk](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/rip-omnivore-omnivore-to-shut-down-on-15-november/38868)
- Omnivore's export "left a lot to be desired"; people hunted for scripts to get something importable — [search summary incl. HN / gleamr](https://gleamr.io/blog/omnivore-shut-down-alternatives) [search snippet]
- Pocket export arrived as "a series of CSV files" — bayindirh, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44063662)

### Inferences
- "Not as likely to disappear on you", "I never trust anyone to delete my data", "lost their reading list for good" support a message like "Your links live on your phone. There's no server to shut down." Local-only plus automatic backups plus standard bookmarks export is an answer to a fear people have actually been through.
- Importing from Pocket CSV, Omnivore, Raindrop and Instapaper meets a real need, since people are holding export files with nowhere good to put them.
- "Simple and hasn't changed in over 10 years" shows that people value durability and simplicity over features.

### Gaps
- No Android Play Store verbatims about Pocket's shutdown could be retrieved (Play pages didn't render reviews).

## 3. Subscriptions vs one-time purchase

### Takeaway
"Subscription fatigue" is mainstream language in 2025. For small utilities, users say they would rather pay once and see subscriptions as a "cash-grab". "No forced subscription" came up as a buying criterion in the Pocket replacement thread.

### Cited Findings
- "Increasing membership fees and the absence of one-time purchases lead to subscription fatigue among consumers."; "Many users would prefer one-time payments that offer lifetime access to purchased apps."; subscriptions "feel like a cash-grab attempt"; "You're also at the mercy of the app makers since they can change or remove certain features at the drop of a hat." — [Android Police via Yahoo Tech, Apr 5, 2025](https://tech.yahoo.com/articles/why-many-android-apps-monthly-091110226.html)
- Raindrop reviews: "Paid for a year subscription and am questioning the utility of this app"; "Pro plan can feel expensive for basic needs"; "I don't need anything complicated or subscription-based" — [search summary of Raindrop reviews: Google Play / Product Hunt / productivity-apps.com](https://www.producthunt.com/products/raindrop-io/reviews) [search snippet, exact source of each line unverified]
- "no forced subscription" cited as valued — [Product Hunt Pocket thread](https://www.producthunt.com/p/pocket/pocket-is-shutting-down-best-read-it-later-alternatives)
- dod9er: "I would love a cheap alternative." — [HN, 2024](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41985118)

### Inferences
- Copy angle: "Pay once. Keep it." / "No subscription. No account. No server." The fact that local-only means no server costs also explains why a one-time price is sustainable, which answers the "how will they survive?" distrust (vikrantsingh_au).

### Gaps
- No verbatim Reddit "pay once" quotes retrieved.

## 4. Accounts, cloud and privacy

### Takeaway
"No account" and "local only" are now standard selling points among small Android bookmark apps, which suggests they resonate. The strongest user language is about trust and data ownership after shutdowns, more than surveillance-style privacy.

### Cited Findings
- Competitor copy: "fully offline, no account required" (Bookmark It) — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.code86.bookmarkit); "No account, no Google lock-in — open it and start saving"; "Your links live on your phone, not a Google server." (Tuckii) — [Tuckii blog](https://tuckii.app/blog/best-bookmark-app-for-android); "Your data stays on your device by default." (Save It Later) — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.saveitlater.app&hl=en_US) [search snippets of store copy]
- "I never trust anyone to delete my data; I'm happier doing that myself." — dario, [MPU 2024](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/rip-omnivore-omnivore-to-shut-down-on-15-november/38868)
- Strong self-hosting pull: "Since I'm slowly moving to self-hosting, I'll be installing Wallabag most probably." — bayindirh, [HN 2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44063662)

### Inferences
- Several direct competitors already say "no account" and "local", so Link Bucket can't win on that alone. Pair it with the queue concept and the widget.
- People who prefer to self-host are a natural audience for local-only. "Self-hosted without the server" is a possible angle.

### Gaps
- No verbatim user quote containing "I don't want another account" was found.

## 5. Home-screen widget demand

### Takeaway
There is little direct evidence. One Raindrop review snippet asks for widgets, and an open-source read-later client added home-screen widgets in a PR. No sizable Reddit or review demand was found. A widget looks like a differentiator rather than something people are asking for.

### Cited Findings
- Raindrop Android review: "Some widgets would be nice though" — [search summary of Raindrop Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.raindrop.raindropio&hl=en_US) [search snippet, unverified]
- Open-source reader "liseur" PR: "feat: Add home-screen widgets for reading" — [GitHub PR #249](https://github.com/chmouel/liseur/pull/249)
- Product Hunt user keeping links visible by leaving "Chrome windows opened on my smartphone, during weeks" — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/pocket/pocket-is-shutting-down-best-read-it-later-alternatives) (indirect: people want saved items in sight)

### Inferences
- The widget's value is best framed around the graveyard problem ("out of sight, out of mind" turned around: your queue sits on your home screen). The Chrome-tabs workaround shows that people already keep things visible on purpose.

### Gaps
- Could not search Reddit for "watch later widget" requests. Treat widget demand as unvalidated.

## 6. Use cases (articles, videos, recipes, social links)

### Takeaway
Articles dominate read-later talk, with videos a strong second (Pocket's own pitch: "articles, stories, and videos"). Recipes from TikTok and Instagram Reels make up a large, separate segment with its own "saved folder graveyard" complaint.

### Cited Findings
- "Pocket has been my favorite read-it-later app for saving articles, stories, and videos to read later." — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/pocket/pocket-is-shutting-down-best-read-it-later-alternatives)
- Upworthy's saves ranged "from carpentry to Japanese train stations" — [Upworthy 2025](https://www.upworthy.com/pocket-saying-goodbye/)
- "A 2025 survey found that 40 percent of adults aged 18 to 34 discover new recipes primarily through TikTok, Instagram Reels, or YouTube Shorts"; Instagram's Saved tab "is where recipes go to disappear" — [search summary of recipe-app blogs](https://trypeel.app/blog/how-to-save-tiktok-recipes) [search snippet, survey source not identified; treat as vendor claim]
- A cluster of 2025–26 apps (Peel, FoodiePrep, ReelsMeals, Social Spork, Mise) exists solely to rescue recipes from social links — [search results](https://thepantrybutler.com/blog/save-recipes-from-tiktok-instagram)

### Inferences
- Lead with articles + YouTube; mention recipes, Reels/TikToks, products and places as secondary examples. "That recipe from Reels" is a relatable line for store screenshots.

### Gaps
- No data ranking the use cases by how often they're mentioned; products and places weren't found in sources.

## 7. Objections to a 3-link free plan and how to frame it

### Takeaway
I found no direct user reactions to a 3-item cap. Based on the evidence, heavy savers (32K–37K saves, "personal archive") will see it as stingy. Meanwhile, vendor content argues that unlimited free queues *cause* abandonment, which gives a principled framing.

### Cited Findings
- Unlimited free storage "paradoxically increase[s] abandonment because queues grow without friction" — [Burn451, 2026](https://www.burn451.cloud/blog/read-it-later-app)
- Archivist mindset: "It's like my own personal archive" — [Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/05/22/shutting-down-pocket/); "That's not the point" (of reading) — [Upworthy](https://www.upworthy.com/pocket-saying-goodbye/)
- "read it now or read it never" — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41985118)

### Inferences
- Frame the cap as a design choice, not a paywall: e.g. "Three slots. On purpose." / "A queue you'll actually finish." Make Pro "for collectors who want room" (a one-time unlock), so the cap reads as a philosophy with an escape hatch.
- Expect the objection "3 is useless / just a trial". Get ahead of it by being upfront that the limit is the point and that Pro is a one-time purchase.

### Gaps
- No examples found of competitors with a deliberately tiny free queue, or user reactions to one.
